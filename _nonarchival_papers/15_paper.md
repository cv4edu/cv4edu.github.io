**Abstract**: AI agents that monitor collaborative learning must detect reasoning failures in real time: misconceptions spreading unchecked, weak claims accepted without scrutiny, or participants reasoning actively but reaching wrong conclusions. Building detection models requires datasets where these failures actually occur and are labeled and yet existing collaborative datasets capture only idealized cooperation under shared epistemic goals. Detecting these failures also requires participant-relative signals such as gaze direction, attention targets, and social cues from each person's viewpoint that exocentric sensing cannot reliably recover. We present a testbed addressing both gaps, combining a three-layer annotation framework (communicative acts, player-reported belief states yielding first- and second-order Theory of Mind data, and post-hoc epistemic vigilance assessments) with multi-person egocentric capture via Meta Aria research glasses alongside exocentric recording. Using a social deduction game as a structured environment where unreliable information enters group reasoning with known provenance, we instrument every participant, yielding synchronized first- and third-person streams. Pilot data demonstrates that failed vigilance, belief fragmentation, and traceable misinformation effects emerge naturally and are captured by the framework.
